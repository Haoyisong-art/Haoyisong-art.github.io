Professional Artist

Artist Statement

My work investigates how women's bodies become sites of emotional governance, where pain, discipline, and transformation are moralized and socially coded. I use everyday objects, beds, chairs, pillows, garments, as metaphors for endurance, containment, and relational spaces.

Painting is integral to my practice: through transparency, erasure, repetition, and layered surfaces, I allow the canvas to become an archive, a memory, and a testimony. At the same time, I explore how different media articulate meaning in distinct ways.

Grounded in cross-cultural perspectives, my art highlights both shared human experiences and cultural specificities in how bodies, identities, and emotional regimes are constructed.

Research & Creative Practice

VPAS Doctoral Program, University of Texas at Dallas

My research interests lie at the intersection of Chinese gender history, visual culture, and body studies. I investigate how women's bodies are governed not just by rules, but through the management of emotion and sensation, a process I term affective governance. My work focuses on the sentient body: the body that feels pain, pride, shame, and hope, and reveals how these feelings become the very medium of social and political control.

Doctoral Project

Affective Governance: Shame, Pride, and the Sentient History of the Chinese Female Body

This project traces how dominant ideologies are inscribed upon the body through a carefully managed emotional economy. It examines key historical and contemporary sites of bodily transformation, including:

  • The pious suffering of self-harm for virtue
  • The aesthetic pride and social anxiety of footbinding
  • The biopolitical fear surrounding state reproductive policies
  • The aspirational hope driving the cosmetic surgery market

Artistic Practice

My artistic practice translates this scholarly inquiry into embodied experience. Through interactive installations, painting, and sculpture, I explore how invisible norms of gender and power are made tangible.

My primary creative work, Ritualized Lives of Chinese Women, is an interactive installation that uses motion and voice tracking to immerse participants in the restrictive bodily disciplines prescribed by historical Confucian gender codes, bridging historical analysis with contemporary sensory engagement.
